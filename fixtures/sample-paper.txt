Attention Is All You Need: A Practical Re-implementation Note

Abstract. The Transformer architecture has become the dominant approach for sequence modeling. In this note we re-examine the original encoder-decoder design, the scaled dot-product attention mechanism, and the multi-head attention layer, and we report reproducible results on a small machine translation benchmark.

1. Introduction. Recurrent neural networks and long short-term memory networks were long the standard for natural language processing. They process tokens sequentially, which limits parallelism. The Transformer instead relies entirely on self-attention, removing recurrence and convolution from the encoder and decoder stacks.

2. Method. Each layer applies multi-head attention followed by a position-wise feed-forward network. Residual connections and layer normalization are used around every sub-layer. The model is trained with the Adam optimizer using a warm-up learning rate schedule and label smoothing as a regularization technique.

3. Evaluation. We evaluate with the BLEU score on the WMT English-German translation task. We also report perplexity on a held-out validation set. Ablation studies remove the positional encoding, reduce the number of attention heads, and replace layer normalization with batch normalization.

4. Results. The big variant reaches 28.4 BLEU, outperforming the previous state of the art by more than two BLEU points. Training used data augmentation with back-translation and dropout of 0.1 to reduce overfitting. Gradient clipping was applied at a norm of 1.0.

5. Discussion. Self-attention provides a constant path length between any two positions, which improves the modeling of long-range dependencies. However, the quadratic complexity in sequence length remains a limitation for long documents. Future work includes sparse attention, linear attention, and retrieval-augmented generation.

6. Conclusion. The Transformer is a general-purpose sequence model. With careful tuning of the learning rate, batch size, and warm-up steps, it generalizes well across tasks including machine translation, text classification, and semantic segmentation of documents.
