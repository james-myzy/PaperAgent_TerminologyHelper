SkyJEPA: Learning Long-Horizon World Models for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Control of Quadrotors

Pratyakh Rao¹, Wancong Zhang², Randall Balestriero³, Yann LeCun², Giuseppe Loianno¹
¹NYU Tandon School of Engineering　²FAIR at Meta　³Brown University

Abstract—Accurate dynamics models are critical for informed decision-making in robotic systems, particularly for agile aerial vehicles operating under uncertainty. Neural network dynamics models are attractive for capturing complex nonlinear effects, but existing predictive approaches struggle with long-horizon forecasting because their autoregressive rollout01 mechanism amplifies errors over time.

Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)02 offer a compelling alternative by modeling dynamics in latent space03, yet prior JEPA-style methods for robot navigation have been studied primarily for kinematic-level planning04, with limited investigation in high-frequency control05.

In this work, we introduce the JEPA-style model for real-time quadrotor control06. The proposed approach combines a latent dynamics model07 with a novel physics-inspired prober08 that maps frozen latents to interpretable state09, enabling physically grounded long-horizon prediction without task-specific reward engineering.

We evaluate the approach on aggressive trajectory tracking in simulation and deploy zero-shot on a physical platform, reporting tracking error, recovery rate, and inference latency at 100 Hz.

2. Method

The world model is trained entirely in latent space03: an encoder maps observations to embeddings, a predictor models their evolution under actions, and the prober decodes physical state. No pixel reconstruction is required, which keeps the training signal focused on dynamics rather than appearance.
